Design, QA, migration, and a new language looked unrelated until they met inside House Duck.

  1. 1998

    It began in Korea

    I was curious about how people read objects and screens. That question led me to industrial design.

  2. DESIGN

    Industrial design

    I learned to ask who something is for, why it exists, and how it should feel before asking how it should look.

  3. 3 YEARS

    From a major Korean game company to startups

    Across companies at very different stages, I saw growth and change, success and setbacks up close. In QA and release teams, I learned the practical craft that helps a team carry a product all the way to players.

  4. GERMANY

    From Korea to Germany

    Building a life in a new country and learning German could fill every day. The question of making a game myself never went away.

Three concentrated years

Every bug contains
a piece of a player's time.

QA can look like the job of finding what is wrong. In practice, it meant watching where a player stops, what they misunderstand, and the exact moment they stop caring.

I learned how many decisions stand between a good idea and a finished game—and how quickly a team drifts when it loses its center. That is why House Duck treats the player's time as something precious, even at a small scale.

“A Korean saying tells us that even a dog beside a school learns to recite poetry after three years.
I decided to turn those three years of watching into games of my own.”
